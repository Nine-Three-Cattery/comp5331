## Paper and released experiment

Equation 1 describes a bivariate Gaussian likelihood for future positions. Equations 2 and 5 describe inverse spatial distance and normalized adjacency. Figure 2 keeps an embedding for every pedestrian through the temporal extrapolator. The authors use eight observed frames, twelve future frames and twenty samples. Table 1 selects one graph block and five extrapolator layers. Table 2 gives mean best-of-twenty ADE/FDE of 0.44/0.75 m across the authors' five scenes. Table 3 reports about 7,600 parameters. Section 6 uses SGD for 250 epochs and lowers the learning rate from 0.01 to 0.002 after epoch 150.

The released training program supplies position **increments** to both the graph builder and network and the released evaluator integrates sampled increments. Two neighboring people moving at exactly the same velocity have zero off-diagonal weight regardless of their physical distance. The graph has unit self-loops. It passes the symmetric normalized Laplacian to the network. At the first observed step, all input increments and the entire Laplacian are zero. We follow the released numerical convention for this experiment and evaluate its forecasts in meters using the group protocol. ## Full-scene operators

The graph block projects each observed increment to five channels, propagates along the normalized graph and filters over observed time. It adds a learned residual projection of the input. Both paths have batch normalization. The temporal decoder takes the full scene, expands the time channels from eight to twelve, applies three residual refinements and ends with an output convolution. Its 3 × 3 kernels span features and the ordered pedestrian slots. Both changes of tensor shape in the released decoder reinterpret contiguous storage. Transposing those same axes would change predictions even with identical weights. The test driver rearranges the result to twelve frames × pedestrians × five Gaussian parameters. We select a scored target only after those operators run. [Released model](https://github.com/abduallahmohamed/Social-STGCNN/blob/333d3a57b4d2705e129b21aefefa09c79b2b9ae1/model.py) · [Independent implementation](model.py)

The reference creates one more convolution and activation than its training forward ever calls. Its active graph and decoder use 6,254 learned parameters. The extra 1,309 bring the registered count to 7,563. The independent network implements the active operators. The reference check maps the same weights to both networks and compares every output node, gradients and batch-normalization buffers in training and evaluation. It also compares the displacement likelihood and sampled world paths with fixed noise. [Reference check](check_reference.py)

## Course protocol differences

The authors' loader retains pedestrians present throughout all twenty frames and requires at least two pedestrians. Group 7 fixes each target by manifest. Every pedestrian with eight visible positions enters our graph, including a neighbor who leaves immediately after observation. We keep the singleton dimension for a lone walker. Only labeled target futures enter the training objective. The stored coordinates and perturbations follow `causal_v2`; the model rounds its copy to four decimals before forming increments, just as the released data preparation does. Target corruption can change every edge incident to the affected target and both endpoint degrees. The group measures each exported target's error against the unchanged future. See the [training and validation decisions](independent_design.md) for the minibatch boundary correction, checkpoint criterion and timing population.

The earlier experiment reduced each frame to one two-dimensional target message before learning. These are distinct models. Its weights, scores and report remain in the [target-readout review release](https://github.com/Nine-Three-Cattery/comp5331/releases/tag/social-stgcnn-causal-v2-review-v2).
